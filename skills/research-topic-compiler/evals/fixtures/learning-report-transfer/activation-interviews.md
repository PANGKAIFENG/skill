# Frozen activation evidence pack

This synthetic fixture exists only for deterministic Skill evaluation. It is not a claim about a live product.

## Product and current onboarding

The product is a collaboration workspace. Registration asks for company size and role, then presents a blank project page with a primary button labelled "Create project". The form asks for project name, workflow template, team members, and notification preference before the first project becomes visible.

## Behavioral snapshot

- 1,000 verified registrations entered onboarding.
- 620 opened the blank project page.
- 210 opened the create-project form.
- 105 submitted it.
- 72 invited at least one teammate within seven days.
- Mobile visitors opened the form at a lower rate than desktop visitors; the fixture does not establish causation.

## Interview excerpts

- I-01, solo consultant: "I wanted to see an example before deciding what a project meant here."
- I-02, team lead: "Inviting colleagues before I knew whether the workspace fit us felt risky."
- I-03, operations manager: "The workflow template names assumed I already understood the product."
- I-04, designer who completed activation: "I chose a sample template, changed the title, and understood it once the board appeared."
- I-05, mobile visitor: "The form looked like setup work I should do later on a laptop."
- I-06, team lead who completed activation: "I skipped invitations, created a private draft, then invited the team after adding two tasks."

## Experiment constraints

- The team can change onboarding copy, defer fields, provide a sample project, and alter invitation timing.
- The team cannot change pricing or the core editor during this evaluation.
- A successful experiment must improve first-project creation without reducing seven-day teammate collaboration or increasing support contacts by more than 10%.
- Stop an experiment if the sample size is below the pre-agreed minimum, instrumentation loses more than 2% of events, or the guardrail regresses beyond the limit.

## Evidence boundaries

- Funnel counts show where loss occurs, not why.
- Interviews expose plausible mechanisms but do not estimate population prevalence.
- The mobile correlation may reflect audience mix or context rather than layout.
- No evidence in this pack establishes long-term retention or revenue impact.
